OUR HEART AS A CHURCH

WHAT WE BELIEVE

Our beliefs

These convictions guide our worship, our relationships, and the way we serve.If someone asks, “How can I be saved and receive eternal life?” a Christian answer would be:

We are saved by God’s grace through faith in Jesus Christ—not by earning salvation through good behavior or our own standards of righteousness. Every person has sinned, and sin separates us from God [Romans 3:23; Isaiah 59:2]. God’s standard is holiness, and no one can make themselves right with him by doing enough good deeds [Matthew 5:48; Romans 3:20].

That is why we need a Savior. Jesus died for us while we were still sinners, taking the punishment for sin, and God raised him from the dead [Romans 5:8; 1 Corinthians 15:3–4].
His sacrifice is God’s gift, not something we earn. We are made right with God through faith in Jesus, not by our own works [Ephesians 2:8–9; Romans 4:5].

So the response is to trust Jesus—to believe that he died and rose again, and that his work is sufficient to bring us to God as a chain reaction [John 3:16; Romans 10:9–13].
Repentance means turning toward God and away from sin; it is part of responding to the good news, not a way to earn salvation [Mark 1:15; Acts 3:19]. We come to Christ as we are, acknowledging our need for him [Luke 18:13–14].

Good works do not buy salvation or erase past sins. Rather, they are the fruit of faith and gratitude. God’s grace begins changing the believer’s life, helping them grow in obedience and love [Ephesians 2:10; Titus 2:11–12; Philippians 2:13]. As Scripture says, faith that is alive will be reflected in a person’s actions [James 2:17].

In short: we are saved by grace through faith in Jesus—not by good works. Good works follow as a grateful response to the salvation God gives. [Ephesians 2:8–10]

Think of faith like a seed: it grows life, and over time-that life will produce good fruit—which is good works and good deeds. We are saved by trusting 100% through faith in Jesus sacrifice, not by earning salvation through our merits. Good fruit is the result of legitimate faith. Which ignite deep gratitude within you. Eternal life and salvation is granted to you by your genuine faith not by your good work in good deeds. Because good works in good deeds is the reaction of your true faith [Ephesians 2:8–10; John 15:5].

Jesus came to save sinners. He died for us sinners and took 100% of the hell punishment out of love, even though we did not deserve the free gift [Romans 5:8; 1 Peter 2:24]. When we understand undeserved forgiveness, we want to follow and be good out of gratitude not forcefully. And if it is by force, you are not understanding the good news of forgiveness.
Jesus said people would be recognized by their fruit [Matthew 7:16–20]. That does not mean a person must be perfect or do enough good deeds to earn salvation; it means genuine faith can begin to change how someone lives [Titus 2:11–12]. As your gratitude expands in clarity, you increasingly recognize a desire to follow God’s word freely and to emulate the One who granted you eternal life. Love, rather than law, guides you. This is called the new covenant for Gods children today.

The man who died beside Jesus had no time to be baptized or show a lifetime of good deeds. He admitted his guilt and chained his mind and asked Jesus to remember him after death. Jesus promised him paradise (heaven, eternal life) [Luke 23:39–43]. His story shows that salvation rests on Jesus, not on a person’s ability to complete religious acts to earn a place in heaven. Only by Faith alone.

Baptism is an important act of obedience that shows a person’s grateful commitment to Jesus [Matthew 28:19–20; Acts 2:38]. Christians are baptized because they finally put 100% Faith in the fact that Jesus was a real man 2000 years ago and was proven by more than 500 witnesses that he died and raised Himself to life on the third day and descended into heaven shortly after. And he offers eternal life on those who live by faith alone. Remember, legitimate faith ignite the chain reaction of a grateful heart-which manifest good deeds and works. God continues to invest Grace into your life so that supernaturally you live your life in sanctification after being justified on the day that you declare 100% faith alone in the finish perfect work of Christ.

The church doesn’t give you eternal life. Your own good deeds and good works can’t pay your sins of the past. It’s only by you letting Christ know that you believe (through faith) that Jesus took your hell punishment when he died for you on Calvary [John 3:16].
